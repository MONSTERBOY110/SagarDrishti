Part 1

The problem

No background assumed. Every term used later is introduced here, and the glossary on the last page repeats them.

1.1 Who INCOIS is

The Indian National Centre for Ocean Information Services, Hyderabad, under the Ministry of Earth Sciences. It is the body that tells India what its ocean is doing. It runs India's Tsunami Early Warning Centre and is a UNESCO‑recognised Tsunami Service Provider for the wider Indian Ocean; it issues the daily Ocean State Forecasts behind high wave and swell surge advisories; it supports Coast Guard search and rescue, where knowing which way the water moved at depth decides where to look; and its Potential Fishing Zone advisories reach roughly nine lakh fishing households.

All four jobs turn on one question asked under time pressure: what is the ocean doing right now, and do I believe it?

1.2 The two kinds of ocean data

There are exactly two ways to know, and the gap between them is the whole problem.

A numerical ocean model is a physics simulation. It cuts the ocean into boxes a few kilometres wide and tens of levels deep and solves equations of motion and heat for each one. The output is complete and gap‑free: a temperature, a salinity and a water velocity for every longitude, latitude, depth and time. It is also a guess. A good one, but a guess.

An in situ observation is a real instrument in real water reporting a real number. True, and almost empty: a handful of points in an ocean. The problem statement names four kinds.

InstrumentWhat it is
Argo floatA metre‑long cylinder that drifts at about 1000 m, then every ten days sinks to 2000 m and rises, recording temperature and salinity the whole way up, transmitting by satellite at the surface. About four thousand are active worldwide, each with a unique WMO identifier. One rise is a profile or cast: a column of measurements at one place and time.
BGC floatThe same machine with more sensors: dissolved oxygen, chlorophyll, nitrate, pH and particle backscatter.
GliderA winged robot that changes its buoyancy to fly up and down in a sawtooth, steered from shore. It surveys a chosen line instead of drifting.
CTD castThe oldest method. A ship stops and lowers an instrument on a wire. Conductivity, Temperature, Depth, because salinity is computed from conductivity rather than measured.
The central tension

The model is complete but possibly wrong. The observations are correct but almost empty. A forecaster issuing a cyclone advisory must hold both in mind and judge whether today's run can be trusted in the place that matters. Supporting that judgement is the job.

The formats. Model output and most observations arrive as NetCDF, a self‑describing binary format for multidimensional arrays, written to the CF Conventions (Climate and Forecast). Older CTD work arrives as plain delimited text. A platform that wants to read what INCOIS holds must handle all of it.

1.3 What the problem statement says

SIH26067 was written by INCOIS: "Develop a web-based interactive 3D visualization platform that integrates numerical ocean model outputs and in-situ observations." It is unusually direct about what is wrong. In their words: "no integrated, web-based 3D visualization platform currently exists" that renders model fields and instrument observations "in a single interactive environment"; existing tools are "desktop-bound, support only 2D plan views, or lack the ability to co-visualize model outputs alongside instrument profiles"; forecasters are "forced to toggle between disparate software packages"; and this "impedes timely hazard assessment, search-and-rescue support, fishery advisories, climate monitoring."

The complaint is not aesthetic. The claim is that the missing tool costs time in the four operations that save lives and livelihoods.

Part 2

What exists, and why it is not enough

A project claiming a gap has to prove the gap. We reviewed the landscape before writing code, and one finding changed how we talk about the project.

The correction we make first

INCOIS already runs Digital Ocean (2020), whose launch material claims "3D and 4D (3D in space with time animation) data visualization" on "a georeferenced 3D Ocean." An INCOIS judge knows that sentence, so we never claim no 3D exists at INCOIS. Our position is narrower and defensible: Digital Ocean is a data management portal whose 3D is a georeferenced globe with draped layers and time animation. We build with it, consuming INCOIS's own services, and add what it does not do.

2.3 The four rebuttals, rehearsed

"Digital Ocean already says 3D and 4D." Correct, and we build with it. It is a data management portal with a draped‑layer globe. We add true volumetrics, a quantitative skill panel and language control, while consuming INCOIS's own ERDDAP and THREDDS, so zero data duplication.

"MyOcean Pro already views four dimensions." Its depth axis is a selector, one level at a time. Copernicus's own roadmap puts their 3D globe at January 2027, and even that is without volumetrics or in situ integration.

"pyParaOcean already does this." Yes, and we cite it. It is also the definition of the desktop‑bound tool the problem statement complains about. We deliver a defensible subset at a URL with zero install, plus the observation comparison and language layers it has no scope for.

"Is this not nullschool for the ocean?" Those are surface particle animations. No in situ data, no export, no skill metric, no column.

Part 3

What SagarDrishti is

Sagar Drishti means "ocean vision". The name echoes the SAGAR doctrine and the Deep Ocean Mission, because the audience is a ministry.

A web page you open with no installation, showing the Indian Ocean as a three‑dimensional body of water rather than a flat map. The simulated field is drawn through the whole depth of the column. The real instruments floating in that water are drawn inside it as marks you can click. A panel tells you how far the simulation is from those instruments, in degrees. The live government disaster warnings are drawn over the water they concern. And a question box answers in sentences built only from numbers the software actually computed.

The core USP, in four claims

1. The water column is drawn as a volume, in a browser, with the real instruments inside it

Not a surface map with a depth dropdown. The field is rendered through depth using the three techniques the problem statement names: stacked depth slices, extracted isosurfaces, and animation through time. Argo, BGC, glider and CTD casts sit in the same 3D space, told apart by mark shape, and clicking one opens its measured profile beside the model's curve at the same place and time.

2. The viewer tells you whether to trust what it is showing you

The differentiator a domain expert notices first. Every operational centre produces skill statistics; they are computed offline and published as quarterly PDFs. Nobody puts the number next to the picture. We do, by GODAE OceanView Class 4, the accepted international protocol, which works in observation space: the model is interpolated to each instrument's exact latitude, longitude, depth and time rather than both being averaged onto a convenient grid, and bias and RMSE are reported per depth band using Argo quality flags 1 and 2 only.

3. The disaster warnings are on the same globe as the physics

The theme is Disaster Management and the first stated mandate is "timely hazard assessment." HazardWatch reads CAP bulletins, the Common Alerting Protocol, which is the international XML standard for public warnings and the format India's national SACHET system speaks. Each becomes a coloured area on the water, severity driving colour, so the warning and the field explaining it are in one picture.

4. You can ask it questions, and it cannot make anything up

Samudra Sahayak, "ocean helper", is the question box. The design constraint is the interesting part: it is structurally unable to compute or invent a number. Eight tools, each calling the same public API a user could open themselves. Numbers enter an answer only as a tool result, and every answer carries the dataset, the timestamp and the float identifiers it used.

The rest of the product

Six guided tours drive the real scene rather than playing a video, serving the outreach and education mandate the problem statement asks for and doubling as a demo that cannot go wrong. It runs with the network unplugged: offline is the default posture, not a mode, so a demonstration can be given with the WiFi visibly off, which turns "deployable on INCOIS infrastructure" into something the audience watches happen. SagarNode is a tabletop ESP32 rig with a temperature probe, a turbidity sensor and a TDS probe used as a conductivity‑derived salinity proxy, which is what it honestly is. It appears on the globe as a live virtual mooring; warm water poured in trips a threshold and fires a CAP‑shaped alert flagged as coming from a demo sensor. It demonstrates requirement F6 with hardware instead of a paragraph.

Part 4

How it works

Everything named here exists in the repository.

4.1 The most important design decision: two planes

The data plane is deterministic: files become arrays, arrays become a chunked store, the store is served over HTTP, the browser draws it. It satisfies all seven requirements on its own. The agent plane is orchestration, and it reaches the data plane over the same public HTTP API the browser uses, through nothing else.

The split is enforced, not merely described. The agent service's dependency list contains no array library, no storage library and no database driver, so it physically cannot read a data file even by mistake, and therefore cannot know anything a user could not verify by opening the same URL. Cut the agent out and the product still meets the specification.

4.2 Sources and the registry

Fourteen sources declared, thirteen enabled, across nine kinds, all in one file, data/sources.yaml. That file is the extensibility surface F3 and F6 ask for: adding a variable or a source is an edit to it, not a code change.

4.3 Ingestion, where correctness is actually decided

Reading scientific files correctly is most of the difficulty, and it is where the tests are concentrated. Four problems recur:

  • CF decoding. A variable may be stored as small integers with a scale_factor and add_offset to apply and a _FillValue marking absent data. Wrong order, or treating the fill value as real, gives a plausible field that is wrong.
  • The sign of depth. Some files count depth positive downwards, some negative. Backwards silently inverts the ocean.
  • Pressure is not depth. Instruments record decibars. The convenient one‑decibar‑equals‑one‑metre approximation is wrong by enough to matter: at 1000 dbar at five degrees north, true depth is 992.08 m. We convert through TEOS-10, which is latitude aware, and the value is pinned in a test.
  • Quality control. We keep Argo QC flags 1 and 2 and discard the rest. A subtlety found the hard way: a level whose pressure flag is bad is unusable even if its temperature flag is good, because we no longer know where the measurement was taken. That rule is enforced across every parameter.

Output is a Zarr store, NetCDF array data re‑chunked so a browser can fetch one block over HTTP instead of a whole file, plus a Parquet table of 30,012 quality‑controlled levels.

4.4 The API, and the four plugins

FastAPI in Python, sixteen routes: /catalog, /field, /isosurface, /currents, /profiles and /profiles/{id}, /scorecard, /warnings, /storyboards, /plugins and /plugins/reload, /ingest/sagarnode and /sagarnode, /healthz, plus /wms and /wcs. That last pair is requirement F7 and the strongest deployment argument in the project: because the platform speaks WMS 1.3.0 and WCS 1.0.0, the desktop software INCOIS already runs can read from it on installation day, with no adapter to write.

4.5 The browser

Next.js 15, React 19 and TypeScript; CesiumJS for the globe, deck.gl for custom WebGL layers, ECharts for profile plots, and Zustand holding one serializable scene state object that the interface, deep links and the agent all mutate through the same door. Volume rendering follows the performance budget in order: stacked depth‑slice textures first because they always work, server‑precomputed isosurface meshes second, GPU ray marching last and only where the frame rate holds. Measured on the demo laptop: 60 frames per second at 1920 by 1080 on the integrated chip, against a target of 60 and a floor of 30.

4.6 The agent, HazardWatch, SagarNode, offline

Eight agent tools, each a thin call to a route above: search_catalog, get_field_summary, find_profiles, get_profile, compare_model_obs, get_warnings, list_storyboards, and set_scene, which is the only way the agent can affect what is drawn. A guard runs over every finished answer regardless of what produced it, and a question outside the tool set gets a refusal listing what the tools can actually do.

HazardWatch turns a parsed CAP bulletin into an event, severity, urgency, certainty, polygon and validity window, drawn as a coloured area with a pulsing border for immediate urgency. The 15 bulletins were reduced from roughly 114,000 polygon vertices to 3,390 by simplification at a hundredth of a degree, which is what makes them cheap enough to draw every frame.

SagarNode is about 230 lines of ESP32 sketch sampling a DS18B20 probe, a TDS probe and a turbidity sensor at one hertz with a ten‑sample median filter, publishing JSON over MQTT with an HTTP POST fallback because campus WiFi frequently blocks the MQTT port. It is registered in the same YAML file as every other source. A rolling z‑score trips a threshold and publishes onto the same CAP bus HazardWatch reads.

Offline is the default: the OFFLINE environment variable defaults to 1, so you opt in to touching the network rather than out. The API has no HTTP client in its dependency list and an assertion that fails if one appears. Exactly one file in the repository reaches the network, tools/fetch_sample.py, and the running service never imports it.

The one thing most likely to be misread

Every answer the question box gives is tagged planner: rules in the interface. A demo that lets an audience believe a language model is reasoning when a regular expression is matching has misled them, and a reviewer who works that out unprompted has caught us rather than been told. The reason it is rules today is practical: an air‑gapped demo rules out a cloud model, no local model server is installed, and a small local model doing reliable tool calling is a week of work with a real chance of failing on stage. What matters is that the shape is real. The tools are the tools a model would be handed, with the schemas it would be handed, and the guard that stops fabrication already runs on every answer. When a model lands it replaces one function, which means the safety layer will have been in production for weeks before the thing it guards against becomes possible.

Reference

Glossary

ADCP
Acoustic Doppler Current Profiler. Measures water speed at many depths using sound.
Argo float
A drifting robot that sinks to about 2000 m and rises every ten days, recording temperature and salinity. Roughly four thousand active worldwide.
BGC float
A biogeochemical Argo float, adding oxygen, chlorophyll, nitrate, pH and backscatter.
Bias and RMSE
Two ways of scoring a forecast. Bias is the average signed error, so it says whether the model runs warm or cold. RMSE is the typical error size regardless of sign.
CAP
Common Alerting Protocol. An OASIS standard XML format for public warnings, carrying event, severity, urgency, certainty and area. India's backbone for it is NDMA SACHET.
CF Conventions
Climate and Forecast. The agreed rules for how a NetCDF file describes its own contents.
Class 4
The GODAE OceanView protocol for verifying forecasts in observation space, interpolating the model to each observation's exact position, depth and time.
CTD cast
Conductivity, Temperature, Depth. An instrument package lowered from a ship. Salinity is computed from conductivity.
Digital twin
A continuously updated computational model of a physical system, detailed enough to be interrogated in its place.
ERDDAP
A scientific data server exposing the same dataset in many formats over HTTP. INCOIS runs one, and we consume it rather than duplicating its data.
Glider
A winged underwater robot that changes buoyancy to fly up and down in a sawtooth, surveying a chosen line.
HF radar
Shore radar measuring surface currents out to about a hundred kilometres.
INCOIS
Indian National Centre for Ocean Information Services, Hyderabad, under the Ministry of Earth Sciences. Author of the problem statement.
In situ
"In position". A measurement taken by an instrument physically in the water, as opposed to a simulation or satellite estimate.
Isosurface
The surface joining every point of equal value in a volume. The 26 degree isosurface bounds the water a cyclone feeds on.
Marching cubes
The standard algorithm for extracting an isosurface from a 3D grid.
Mooring
An instrumented line anchored to the seabed, reporting from fixed depths. RAMA is the Indian Ocean array.
NetCDF
Network Common Data Form. The standard self‑describing binary format for multidimensional scientific arrays.
OGC WMS, WCS
Open Geospatial Consortium standards. WMS returns a rendered map picture, WCS returns the underlying numbers. Speaking both means existing GIS software reads our data unmodified.
Parquet
A columnar table format, efficient to filter and read in parts. It holds our observation levels.
Profile, cast
One column of measurements at a single place and time.
QC flag
Quality control flag, recording how far a measured value can be trusted. We keep 1 and 2, good and probably good.
Reanalysis
A model run over the past with all observations available, giving the best reconstruction of what the ocean did. GLORYS is the Copernicus one.
TEOS-10
Thermodynamic Equation of Seawater 2010. Used here to convert pressure to depth and to derive density.
Thermocline
The depth band where temperature falls fastest, separating the warm surface layer from the cold deep. Our model error is largest there.
Volumetric rendering
Drawing the interior of a 3D body of data rather than its surface or a flat slice.
WMO identifier
The World Meteorological Organization number uniquely identifying an instrument. Ours appear in every citation.
Zarr
A format storing an array as many small chunk files, so a browser can fetch one block over HTTP instead of a whole dataset.
